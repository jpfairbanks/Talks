Optimal Parts, Suboptimal Whole

A heterogeneous flock is sent to reach a stranded vehicle and observe its surroundings. Before launch it must decide:
Each decision limits the others. Analyzed apart, optimal subsystems compose into a suboptimal whole, and stable ones into an unstable one.
2
The problem the services care about: a mixed team of air, surface and undersea vehicles, with different dynamics, sensors and radios, given one mission. The decisions are coupled, and each is usually analyzed alone. This talk is about the mathematics that lets us analyze them together and still compute locally.

Graph-Laplacian Control, and Its Assumptions

Agents are nodes, sensing or communication links are edges. Each agent compares its state with its neighbors', and local disagreement drives the network toward a global objective.
  • ●Compatible state spaces across every agent
  • ●A measurement each pair of neighbors can compare directly
  • ●Two planar robots comparing positions in the same frame
Natural for consensus, formation control, and leader–follower tracking — as long as the states are comparable.
4
The baseline: consensus on a graph. It assumes that neighbors can compare their states directly.

Real Systems Are Heterogeneous

  • ●Agents and targets need not live in the same dimension
  • ●Only partial or projected measurements may be available
  • ●Sensing models differ across a fleet
  • ●The graph records who talks to whom, and nothing about what they can compare
[1] T. Hanks, H. Riess, S. Cohen, T. Gross, M. Hale, and J. Fairbanks. IEEE Conference on Decision and Control, 2025
5
That assumption fails for multi-domain teams. A drone and a submarine do not share a state space; the graph says who talks, not what they can compare. Skip this slide if the clock is tight.

Cellular Sheaves Over Graphs

Definition (Cellular Sheaf)
A cellular sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) over a graph \(G = (V, E)\) consists of:
  • A vertex stalk (vector space) \(\mathcal{F}(v)\) for each vertex \(v \in V\)
  • An edge stalk (vector space) \(\mathcal{F}(e)\) for each edge \(e \in E\)
  • A linear restriction map \(\mathcal{F}_{v \unlhd e}\colon \mathcal{F}(v) \to \mathcal{F}(e)\) for each incident pair \(v \unlhd e\)
[2] J. Hansen and R. Ghrist. Journal of Applied and Computational Topology, 3(4):315–358, 2019
6
Put a vector space on every agent and every link, and a linear map that translates each agent's state into the link's space, where the two agents' states can be compared. That is a cellular sheaf.

Three Agents Tracking One Target

Every agent stalk is ℝ², every edge stalk ℝ: each edge compares the target against the line its agent moves along. This is the sheaf behind a Robotarium demo.
7
A concrete one: agents on three lines, a target inside. Each link compares the target with the line the agent moves on, so edge stalks are one-dimensional. Diffusion slides each agent along its line, and the Dirichlet energy falls to zero at the equilibrium: the perpendicular feet. This is the sheaf behind the Robotarium demo.

Sheaf Laplacians Compute Global Sections

The sheaf Laplacian is \(L_\mathcal{F} \triangleq \delta^\top \delta\) where \((\delta \mathbf{x})_{ij} \triangleq \mathcal{F}_{i \unlhd ij} x_i - \mathcal{F}_{j \unlhd ij} x_j\)
Graph Laplacian:
\[ (L_G \mathbf{x})_i = \sum_{j \in N_i} (x_i - x_j) \]
Sheaf Laplacian:
\[ (L_\mathcal{F} \mathbf{x})_i = \sum_{j \in N_i} \mathcal{F}_{i \unlhd ij}^\top \left( \mathcal{F}_{i \unlhd ij}(x_i) - \mathcal{F}_{j \unlhd ij}(x_j) \right) \]
Proposition (Global Sections as Kernel). The space of global sections satisfies \(\Gamma(G; \mathcal{F}) \triangleq \ker \delta = \ker L_\mathcal{F}\).
Theorem (Sheaf Diffusion Convergence). Sheaf diffusion \(\dot{\mathbf{x}} = -L_\mathcal{F} \mathbf{x}\) converges to the orthogonal projection of \(\mathbf{x}(0)\) onto \(\Gamma(G; \mathcal{F})\).
The operator that drives consensus on a graph has an exact analogue for heterogeneous agents, and its kernel is the space of global sections.
[3] J. Hansen. Laplacians of Cellular Sheaves: Theory and Applications, PhD dissertation, University of Pennsylvania, 2020
9
The sheaf Laplacian is the graph Laplacian with the translation maps in it. Its kernel is the set of globally consistent states, and diffusion converges to it. Every agent computes its own row.

Coordination Sheaves

Each part of a coordination sheaf has a meaning in the multi-agent system:
Definition (Coordination Sheaf). A coordination sheaf on a graph \(G=(V,E)\) is a cellular sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) over \(G\) together with differentiable potential functions \(U_{ij}\colon \mathcal{F}(ij)\to \mathbb{R}\) for each edge \(ij \in E\).
MAS Interpretations for Coordination Sheaves
Sheaf Concept MAS Interpretation
Graph \(G\) Bidirectional communication topology
Vertex stalk \(\mathcal{F}(i)\) State space of agent \(i\)
0-cochains \(C^0(G;\mathcal{F})\) Global state space
Edge stalk \(\mathcal{F}(ij)\) Communication space between \(i\) and \(j\)
Restriction map \(\mathcal{F}_{i \unlhd ij}\) Registers state between agents
Potential \(U_{ij}\) Coordination goal between \(i\) and \(j\)
[1] T. Hanks, H. Riess, S. Cohen, T. Gross, M. Hale, and J. Fairbanks. IEEE Conference on Decision and Control, 2025
10
The dictionary from multi-agent systems to sheaves. The potentials on the edges are how an engineer writes the goal: consensus, a formation offset, a tracking objective.

Homological Programs and Their Guarantees

Definition (Nonlinear Homological Program). Data \(\mathsf{P} = (V, E, \mathcal{F}, \{f_i\}, \{U_e\})\) with graph \(G\), sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\), objectives \(\{f_i\}\), and potentials \(\{U_e\}\):
\[ \begin{aligned} \underset{\mathbf{x} \in C^0(G;\mathcal{F})}{\text{minimize}} \quad & \sum_{i \in V} f_i(x_i) \\ \text{subject to} \quad & L_\mathcal{F}^{\nabla U} \mathbf{x} = 0 \end{aligned} \]
Theorem (Convexity & Tractability). If each edge potential \(U_e\) is differentiable and convex, and each agent objective \(f_i\) is convex, then \(\mathsf{P}\) is a convex optimization problem.
Theorem (Nonlinear Sheaf Diffusion). For single-integrator dynamics \(\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{u} = -L_\mathcal{F}^{\nabla U}\mathbf{x}\) with strongly convex edge potentials, trajectories converge exponentially to \(\arg\min_{\mathbf{x} \in C^0(G;\mathcal{F})} U(\delta_\mathcal{F}\mathbf{x})\).
Theorem (Decentralized ADMM Convergence). For convex \(\mathsf{P}\) with quadratic potentials and a Lagrangian saddle point:
  • Residual convergence: \(\mathbf{x}^k - \mathbf{z}^k \to 0\)
  • Objective convergence: \(\sum_i f_i(x_i^k) + \chi_\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{z}^k) \to p^\star\)
  • Dual variable convergence: \(\mathbf{y}^k \to \mathbf{y}^\star\) (dual optimal point)
Local gradient flow on the sheaf potential computes the global coordination state with provable convergence.
[1] T. Hanks, H. Riess, S. Cohen, T. Gross, M. Hale, and J. Fairbanks. IEEE Conference on Decision and Control, 2025 · [4, 5]
11
First result, CDC 2025, Tyler Hanks's paper. Coordination for heterogeneous agents is a convex program when the parts are convex, and two decentralized algorithms solve it with guarantees: nonlinear sheaf diffusion, exponentially, and ADMM. One formulation covers consensus, formation and tracking.

  • ●Longer delays slow convergence but do not stop it

Who Can Track Whom Is a Cohomological Question

Ships Tracking Submarines — Determined
each submarine fixes one point on the water: where its ship must be
A ship is stuck on the surface, so the target’s three coordinates determine its two. The harmonic extension is unique and the relative cohomology vanishes.
Quadcopters Tracking Ships — Underdetermined
each ship fixes a whole line, a copy of ℝ: its quadcopter can sit anywhere on it
A quadcopter also has an altitude, which nothing it measures about a surface vessel constrains. That spare degree of freedom is the non-vanishing relative cohomology.
Trackability is whether the measurements pin down every degree of freedom the agents have. The cohomology counts the ones they miss.
[7] T. Hanks, C. F. Nino, J. Bou Barcelo, A. Copeland, W. E. Dixon, and J. Fairbanks. European Control Conference, 2026
13
Third, ECC 2026. Whether a team can track a set of targets is a computable algebraic question: the relative cohomology. In both pictures the tracker is on top, trying to get over its target. On the left, projecting a submarine up to the water gives one point, and its ship has to be there. On the right, the ship is the one on the water: every point on the vertical line above it agrees with what the quadcopter measures, so the quadcopter can sit at any altitude on that line. A point against a whole line, a copy of R: that free altitude is the relative cohomology. Ships tracking submarines are determined; quadcopters tracking ships leave each altitude free. The widget on the next slide computes it live.

Sheaf-Laplacian Feedback and Its Guarantee

Local Sheaf Disagreement
\[ \eta_i = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} \mathcal{F}_{i \unlhd ij}^{\top}\big( \mathcal{F}_{i \unlhd ij} q_i - \mathcal{F}_{j \unlhd ij} x_j \big) \]
Decentralized Controller
\[ u_i = -k_1\, g_i^{+} \eta_i \]
Control Gain
\(k_1 > 0\) — a larger gain shrinks the residual tracking error.
Input Map
\(g_i^{+}\) — maps the correction into agent \(i\)'s control space.
Theorem (Semi-global exponential convergence). Under the standing assumptions, the tracking error relative to the harmonic extension converges semi-globally uniformly exponentially to a bounded residual set. A larger gain \(k_1\) shrinks the residual error arbitrarily.
The residual error comes from dynamics mismatch and bounded disturbances, and has the same form as a singular perturbation bound.
Each agent computes \(u_i\) from its neighbours’ measurements. No agent needs the global state.
The ordinary Lyapunov argument for Laplacian feedback, run in the sheaf’s coordinates.
[7] T. Hanks, C. F. Nino, J. Bou Barcelo, A. Copeland, W. E. Dixon, and J. Fairbanks. European Control Conference, 2026
15
When the test passes, a decentralized feedback law built from the sheaf Laplacian tracks the targets: semi-global exponential convergence to a residual set that a larger gain shrinks. Each agent uses only its neighbors' measurements.

Coordination Is a Constrained Harmonic Extension

Partition the vertices into agents \(A\) and targets \(B\). Targets are oblivious: they carry no agent state.
Minimize disagreement over the agents, holding the targets fixed
\[ E(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}) = \tfrac12\lVert \delta_A \mathbf{q} + \delta_B \mathbf{p}\rVert^2 \;\;\longrightarrow\;\; L_A\, \mathbf{q}^\star = -\delta_A^{*}\delta_B\, \mathbf{p} \]
Constrain the formation to a convex \(\mathcal{K}\): a safe set, or actuator limits
\[ \mathbf{q}^\star_0 \;=\; \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{q}\,\in\,\mathcal{K}}\; E(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}) \]
  • ●\(L_A \succ 0\) exactly when the relative cohomology vanishes, \(\mathcal{H}^0(G, B; \mathcal{F}) = 0\) — the fleet is pinned enough to have a unique answer
  • ●One solve per control step. Agent \(i\) then holds only its own block \(\mathbf{q}^\star_{\mu,i}\)
The formation is the solution of an optimization problem, not the output of a feedback law.
[8] I. Kadosh, R. Samuelson, and J. Fairbanks. American Control Conference, 2027, under review · [2, 1, 7]
17
Fourth, Itay Kadosh's paper, under review at ACC 2027: safety. Make the fleet's formation the solution of a convex problem, and keep-out zones or actuator limits become constraints on it. One solve per control step; it has a unique answer exactly when the cohomology test from before passes.

Four directions. Riemannian sheaves: each agent's stalk is a manifold of its own, a sphere, a torus, a saddle, and the restriction maps are smooth maps onto the edges' manifolds, so agents whose states live on different curved spaces can optimize together. Scale: PATHPACK.jl, the same algebra on GPUs. One landscape, three questions (cheapest route, worst barrier, where a wandering animal goes), each one semiring in the same solver; all 4.5 billion pairs in about a tenth of a second on one B200. Hardware: the controllers on robots, with the Robotarium and Matt Hale's lab. Energy: symplectic and metriplectic formulations. The picture is one surface of constant energy, shaded by entropy. The symplectic part moves the state around the dashed entropy levels without changing either; the metric part pushes it up the entropy gradient inside the surface. So energy is conserved exactly and entropy only rises, which is what energy-driven and cyber-physical models need.

Key Results

Heterogeneous coordination is a convex program. Consensus, formation and tracking for agents with different state spaces, solved by local gradient flow or decentralized ADMM, with convergence guarantees. CDC 2025 [1]
Robust to communication delay. Sheaf diffusion still converges when each agent runs on its own clock with bounded delays; longer delays only slow it. ACC 2026 [6]
Software, open source. CellularSheaves.jl and the browser tools shown here; the same controllers moving to Robotarium hardware with Matt Hale’s lab. [9]
Who can track whom is computable. Trackability across domains (air, surface, undersea) is a relative cohomology group, and decentralized sheaf feedback converges semi-globally exponentially. ECC 2026 [7]
Keep-out zones are constraints on the fleet’s solve. One convex solve per control step places the whole formation. Six heterogeneous quadrotors on SE(3) clear two obstacles by 0.100 m and hold formation to 2.0 mm RMS. Under review at ACC 2027 [8]
One sheaf model gives the convex program, the convergence proof, the trackability test and the safety constraint.
